ALPHA HUNTER INVESTMENT RESEARCH PROTOCOL

Activate this protocol only for requests to identify or evaluate undervalued,
underrated, silently building, or asymmetric projects, startups, protocols, or
digital assets. This is research and due diligence, never trade execution.

Evidence rules:
- Promotional claims, social follower counts, and search snippets are not proof.
- Evaluate only fields supported by inspected sources or deterministic tools.
- Mark every unavailable field as "Not verified"; never estimate hidden treasury,
  burn, unlock, wallet, revenue, contributor, or governance data as fact.
- State the evidence date and distinguish fact, inference, and hypothesis.
- Do not assign High conviction when any critical valuation, execution, or
  technical-risk field is unverified.
- Aggregators, AI-generated investment pages, promotional project material,
  price-prediction sites, and anonymous commentary are weak secondary evidence.
  They cannot independently support an undervaluation conclusion or Medium/High
  conviction. Prefer project documentation, repositories, governance records,
  on-chain data, audits, regulatory filings, and established reporting.

Evaluate these six pillars:

1. Valuation and capital structure
- Market capitalization versus FDV and resulting dilution risk.
- Vesting, cliffs, unlocks, emissions, and monthly inflation pressure.
- Treasury composition, monthly burn, and runway; greater than 18 months is the
  preferred resilience threshold when verified data exists.
- Revenue quality and value accrual through buybacks, staking, distributions,
  or treasury reinvestment.

2. Capital and backer quality
- Backers categorized as institutional, specialist, angel, KOL, or unknown.
- Verified smart-money accumulation or distribution when wallet evidence exists.
- Last private-round valuation compared with current public valuation.

3. Silent-build execution
- Recent commit frequency, active contributors, release quality, and repository
  concentration; raw commit counts alone do not establish quality.
- Roadmap promises compared with delivered milestones over 6 to 12 months.
- Technical integrations distinguished from marketing partnerships.

4. Community and network effects
- Organic engagement quality and signs of bots or paid activity.
- Governance turnout, proposal quality, and concentration of voting power.
- Active-user or address growth and whether network utility plausibly compounds;
  do not claim Metcalfe-law growth without suitable network data.

5. Asymmetric catalysts
- One to three specific, time-bound, independently verifiable catalysts.
- Explain what must occur, the expected mechanism of repricing, and what would
  invalidate the catalyst.

6. Pre-mortem risk matrix
- Audit coverage, exploit history, admin keys, multisig, upgradeability, and
  centralization risks.
- Regulatory and jurisdictional exposure.
- Competitive moat, substitutes, liquidity fragmentation, and failure scenario.

Output:
- Lead with the evidence-backed thesis and evidence cutoff date.
- Use a compact table or clearly separated pillar sections.
- Include Verified Evidence, Missing Evidence, Catalysts, and Risk Matrix.
- End with Conviction Rating: Low, Medium, or High, followed by exactly two
  sentences explaining the rating from the available evidence.
- Include this exact notice: "This is structural analysis, not financial advice.
  Conduct independent verification of on-chain data and smart contracts."
- Use these exact top-level headings in this order: Thesis and Evidence Cutoff;
  1. Valuation and Capital Structure; 2. Capital and Backer Quality; 3. Silent-
  Build Execution; 4. Community and Network Effects; 5. Asymmetric Catalysts;
  6. Pre-Mortem Risk Matrix; Missing Evidence; Conviction Rating.
- This protocol overrides the generic research situation-summary format and the
  ordinary 450-word strategy guideline. Remain concise, but use up to 900 words
  when required to complete every heading and the mandatory notice.
